\begin{abstract}
Serializable snapshot isolation must detect a write that would have changed the
answer of a read that already happened. The reader therefore records what it
read as a predicate rather than as a set of rows, and the writer checks whether
its new value falls under any recorded predicate. Where an access method offers
no support, the predicate degenerates to the whole relation and every insertion
conflicts with every scan.

This paper is an account of adding such support to an extensible index
framework, of what the framework's structure contributes to the argument, and of
one question that was asked at the time, judged hypothetical, and turned out
seven years later not to be. The implementation for the generalized search tree
was carried out by Shubham Barai as a Google Summer of Code project under the
present author's supervision, and the subsequent revision for the generalized
inverted index by Heikki Linnakangas; the author's role was design review and
the verification requirements described below, and the paper is written as an
account rather than as a claim of priority.

We set out the covering argument that makes page granularity correct for a
generalized search tree, and why the same granularity, transplanted to the
inverted index, was not sound and had to be revised. We describe the requirement
imposed on the project's isolation tests~--- that each must fail if the access
method does not implement predicate locking at all~--- and argue that this is the
minimum standard for a test of a concurrency-control feature, since a test that
passes either way measures nothing. And we return to a question raised in review
in 2017: whether garbage collection may erase a page holding a predicate lock.
It was correctly set aside then, because the structure did not delete pages. It
began deleting them in 2019, in work by the present author, and the interaction
appears not to have been addressed since.
\end{abstract}
